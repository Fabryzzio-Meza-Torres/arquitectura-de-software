## Functional Requirements — Lea$e (machinery leasing, Peru)

| ID | Functional requirement |
|---|---|
| FR-01 | The system must allow a client company to submit a leasing application capturing RUC, machinery type, requested term in months, requested amount in PEN, and the expected settlement date of the client company's project, persisting it in state `SUBMITTED` in under 2 seconds at p95; if any of these five fields is missing or the RUC fails SUNAT 11-digit format validation, the system must reject the submission with a per-field error message and must not create a partial application. If the submission fails due to loss of connectivity, the draft must be stored locally and retried up to 3 times at 10-second intervals before alerting the client company. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-02 | The system must allow the client company to attach the mandatory credit dossier (last 12 months of bank statements, latest annual tax return, and the awarded-project contract or purchase order) to an application in state `SUBMITTED`, accepting PDF/JPG/PNG files of up to 20 MB each, and must block the transition to `IN_REVIEW` while any of the three mandatory documents is absent, showing which one is missing. If a file fails the antivirus scan it must be quarantined, not stored, and the client company must be notified within 1 minute with the reason. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-03 | The system must request an external credit-bureau score for every application entering `IN_REVIEW` with a request timeout of 10 seconds, retrying twice with exponential backoff (2 s, 4 s); if the bureau is still unavailable after the third attempt, the application must be moved to state `SCORING_UNAVAILABLE` and routed to the leasing company's manual review queue (FR-05) instead of being auto-rejected, and the failure must be recorded in the audit trail of FR-07. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-04 | The system must produce, for every application, a decision of `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `CONDITIONED` within 24 business hours of entering `IN_REVIEW`, storing the approved credit limit in PEN, the maximum term in months, and at least one machine-readable reason code per decision; an application that reaches 20 business hours without a decision must trigger a notification to the leasing company, and one that exceeds 24 business hours must be flagged as `SLA_BREACHED` in the leasing company's manual review queue without altering the application state. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-05 | The system must provide the leasing company with a manual review queue containing every application whose bureau score falls in the configured gray band (default 550–680) or whose state is `SCORING_UNAVAILABLE` (FR-03), ordered by remaining SLA time ascending, and must require a reason code plus a free-text justification of at least 20 characters before allowing any decision to be saved. Applications with a requested amount above 500,000 PEN must additionally require approval through a second, distinct authorized leasing-company account, and the system must block the same account from both reviewing and approving the application. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-06 | The system must notify the client company of the decision on its application (FR-04) through email and in-app inbox within 5 minutes of the decision being saved, including the outcome, the approved limit and term when applicable, and the reason codes in client-readable text; if email delivery fails, the system must retry 3 times over 30 minutes and, regardless of the email outcome, the in-app inbox entry must always be written so that the decision is never lost. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-07 | The system must record, for every state transition of an application, contract, payment schedule and asset, an append-only audit entry containing actor, organization, timestamp with time zone, previous value and new value; audit entries must never be modifiable or deletable by either company, must be queryable by the leasing company filtered by contract, actor and date range with a response under 3 seconds, and must remain retrievable after a failure within the recovery window defined in NFR-02. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-08 | The system must let the client company generate up to 3 comparable payment-schedule simulations for an `APPROVED` application, each configurable with a grace period of 0 to 6 months, a monthly or milestone-based installment frequency, and an optional balloon payment of up to 40 % of the principal payable at the project settlement date; each simulation must show total interest, total cost and the installment calendar, and the sum of installments must equal principal plus interest with any rounding difference under 0.01 PEN absorbed in the final installment. Simulations expire and are purged 30 days after creation. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-09 | The system must let the client company align each installment due date of a chosen simulation (FR-08) to a declared project milestone date, rejecting any configuration where the grace period extends past the project settlement date declared in FR-01 or where an installment falls due before the asset handover date of FR-16, and must display the resulting cash-flow gap in days for every installment. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-10 | The system must activate a leasing contract only when all three conditions hold simultaneously: the application is `APPROVED` (FR-04), the payment schedule is accepted and digitally signed by the client company, and an asset unit is exclusively allocated (FR-14); upon activation the payment schedule is locked and can only change through the restructuring flow of FR-12. If two activation attempts are made for the same application, the second must be rejected with a conflict message and must not create a duplicate contract. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling and Asset lifecycle problems. |
| FR-11 | The system must register incoming payments against a contract's installments using the bank reference as an idempotency key, so that re-processing the same bank reference returns the original receipt without creating a second payment or altering the balance; partial payments must be applied to the oldest outstanding installment first, and any payment that cannot be matched to a contract within 1 business day must be moved to the leasing company's exceptions queue instead of being discarded. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-12 | The system must notify the client company of each upcoming installment through email and SMS 72 hours and again 24 hours before the due date, retrying each channel up to 3 times over 30 minutes on delivery failure; if both channels are exhausted without delivery, the notification must still be written to the in-app inbox and logged as `DEGRADED_DELIVERY` in the audit trail of FR-07. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-13 | The system must apply an automatic dunning ladder to any installment unpaid after its due date, sending reminders at day +1, +8, +15 and +30, transitioning the contract from `ACTIVE` to `OVERDUE` at day +8 and from `OVERDUE` to `DEFAULT` at day +90, and halting the ladder immediately when a payment registered by FR-11 settles the outstanding installment; every transition must notify the client company and the leasing company within 5 minutes and must be recorded in FR-07. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-14 | The system must let the client company request one schedule restructuring per rolling 12-month period for a contract in `ACTIVE` or `OVERDUE` state, stating the reason and the proposed new milestone dates; the leasing company must decide within 5 business days, and an approved restructuring generates a new schedule that supersedes the previous one while the previous schedule and all its payments are retained as historical records and remain visible. A contract in `DEFAULT` is not eligible and the request must be rejected with that reason. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-15 | The system must allocate a specific machinery unit to a contract with an exclusive lock over the requested date range, guaranteeing that no unit is allocated to two contracts with overlapping ranges; a concurrent allocation request for the same unit and overlapping range must fail within 2 seconds with a conflict message and must propose the next available unit of the same type and capacity, or the earliest available date for that unit if no alternative exists. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-16 | The system must record the handover of an allocated unit to the client company's site through a checklist capturing hour-meter reading, at least 4 condition photographs, accessories delivered, and digital confirmation from both the leasing company and the client company; the contract's billing start date must not be set and the schedule of FR-10 must not begin until the checklist is complete and both confirmations are captured. The same checklist structure applies to the return of the unit. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-17 | The system must publish a machinery catalog filterable by type, capacity, branch and date range, showing for every unit its availability calendar at day granularity and its current state (`AVAILABLE`, `ALLOCATED`, `IN_MAINTENANCE`, `IN_RECOVERY`); when no unit matches the requested range the catalog must explicitly show the earliest available date per matching unit instead of an empty result. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-18 | The system must ingest telemetry from each deployed unit every 15 minutes containing GPS position, engine hours and fault codes, and must persist it as the unit's current state; if the unit loses connectivity, the device must buffer readings locally for up to 72 hours and backfill them in chronological order on reconnection, and any unit with no reading for more than 72 hours must raise an exception ticket for the leasing company within 15 minutes of crossing that threshold. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-19 | The system must schedule preventive maintenance for a unit when it reaches every 250 engine hours reported by FR-18 or 6 calendar months since the last service, whichever occurs first, moving the unit to `IN_MAINTENANCE` and excluding it from allocation (FR-15) for the scheduled window; if the maintenance window overlaps an existing allocation, the system must block the automatic scheduling and notify the leasing company to arrange a replacement unit rather than interrupting an active contract. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-20 | The system must open a recovery order for every contract entering `DEFAULT` (FR-13), setting the unit to `IN_RECOVERY`, attaching its last known position and engine hours from FR-18, and assigning it to the leasing company's exceptions queue; the contract may only be closed once the return checklist of FR-16 is completed, and the unit returns to `AVAILABLE` only after a maintenance inspection under FR-19. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling and Asset lifecycle problems. |
| FR-21 | The system must restrict actions and visible data by organization — Client Company and Leasing Company — according to the permission rules defined in NFR-06, protecting the integrity of credit-decision records, payment schedules and asset allocation data; every unauthorized access attempt must be recorded in the audit trail of FR-07 and must return "Unauthorized access" without exposing any of the requested data. Serves all three critical problems as a cross-cutting requirement. |
| FR-22 | The system must let a Broker book a negotiation meeting between the client company and the leasing company for an application in `SUBMITTED` or `IN_REVIEW` state, and must let either company accept, reject or propose a new date; a rejected date must not close the negotiation, and the Broker may propose another. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem (see `Core/MainFlows.md`, Flow 1B). |
| FR-23 | The system must let a Broker upload the contract's PDF (up to 20 MB), a text summary and its structured details against an application; once uploaded, both the client company and the leasing company must be able to view the same document and details. An upload missing the summary or the structured details must be rejected as incomplete. Serves the Credit & risk decisioning problem. |
| FR-24 | The system must let the client company confirm or reject the reception of the allocated machinery unit once delivered by the Provider; a confirmed reception is recorded against the contract, and a rejected reception must be flagged to the leasing company instead of silently passing as confirmed. Serves the Asset lifecycle & availability problem. |
| FR-25 | The system must classify every active contract's delinquency into exactly 4 levels based on elapsed time since the missed due date — Green (paid on time), Yellow (1 month unpaid), Orange (2 months unpaid), Red (more than 2 months unpaid) — recomputing the level daily, and must let the leasing company send a formal message to a client company whose contract is Yellow, Orange or Red. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-26 | The system must compute the pronosticated income of the current month for the leasing company as the sum of every active contract's installment due within the month, assuming full payment, and must expose it as a single figure refreshed at least once per day. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling problem. |
| FR-27 | The system must resolve the end of a contract term through exactly one of two mutually exclusive branches chosen by the client company: the purchase option (allowed only once all remaining installments are paid) or the equipment return (no purchase); the system must reject an attempt to select both, and the resolved contract must move to a closed state and appear in the leasing company's closed-agreements history. Serves the Cash-flow-aligned payment scheduling and Asset lifecycle problems. |
