# Problem

Three critical business problems justify SendIT. Every backlog item must trace to at least
one of them or to a documented user need or flow.

## 1. A cross-border remittance is exposed to fraud and unauthorized payout

Money can be stolen through account takeover, false identities, modified Receiver data,
reused payout credentials or an AgencyWorker acting outside their authority. At the same
time, indiscriminate security checks can strand legitimate users with no explanation.
SendIT must verify the relevant people and transaction, apply least-privilege access,
prevent duplicate or unauthorized money movement, and preserve an auditable explanation for
every hold, rejection, payout, cancellation and refund.

## 2. The promised amount becomes ambiguous between quote, deposit and payout

If the exchange rate changes after the Sender deposits, if a commission appears late, if
rounding is repeated, or if concurrent retries duplicate a charge, the Sender pays an
unexpected amount or the Receiver obtains less than promised. SendIT must disclose all
charges before funding and lock a single monetary snapshot for the entire transaction so
that the Receiver amount actually paid is exactly the amount confirmed.

## 3. Digital and agency processing can fragment one transaction

People with low digital confidence need assisted service, while other users expect web or
mobile self-service. Separate records, manual calculations or inconsistent statuses across
those channels create errors, inaccessible experiences and disputes. SendIT must use one
transaction, one price, one state and one audit timeline across web, mobile and agency
service while keeping the customer—not the AgencyWorker—in control of each decision.
