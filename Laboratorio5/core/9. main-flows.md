# 9. Main flows

## F1. Client report and support resolution

1. Eric receives the client report and registers a `Customer Escalation` ticket with an idempotency key.
2. The system confirms the ID, status, priority, and deadline; a duplicate returns the prior ticket.
3. Eric resolves a user error or consults the Harness. The LLM proposes steps with evidence; Eric reviews, performs, and records the outcome.
4. Only Eric marks the ticket `Resolved` after verifying the outcome; the system notifies stakeholders and invalidates the status cache.

**Alternative:** if information is missing, the system records `Awaiting information`; if the proposal is not useful, continue with F2.

## F2. Escalation to engineering

1. Eric marks the ticket unresolved, or the Harness detects failure or no answer and proposes escalation while retaining its history.
2. The system classifies it as a `Support Escalation`, keeps the client-origin deadline, queues it, and sends Jesus one effective Slack notification through ticket-and-event deduplication.
3. Jesus reviews and assigns it; Julia receives the context, consults permitted sources, and records the human solution or non-resolution.
4. Support and stakeholders receive the status change.

**Alternative:** if Slack fails, the pending event is retried in a bounded manner and then sent to a visible dead-letter queue; the ticket remains in the work queue.

## F3. Engineering incident

1. Julia registers an `Engineering Escalation` and its three-day response deadline.
2. She consults the Harness to retrieve information and a proposal; MCP queries are read-only and trials run in an isolated sandbox.
3. Julia reviews and performs a solution outside the LLM, records the outcome, and, if appropriate, marks it `Resolved`.

**Alternative:** an LLM failure activates fallback and human review without blocking the ticket.

## F4. Erroneous response and reopening

1. Eric or Julia records that the problem persists and reopens the ticket with a reason; the full history is retained.
2. The system invalidates cache, reassesses priority and deadline, and escalates to engineering if the previous answer was erroneous.
3. Jesus assigns the review, and Julia records the new solution or lack of resolution.

## F5. Dangerous proposal or LLM outage

1. Guardrails detects a write, deletion, or sensitive-action request and stops the tool before it executes.
2. The person sees the reason and evidence; they can reject the proposal or authorize an intervention that they perform outside the LLM.
3. Timeout, bounded retries, and circuit breaker provide fallback; after repeated failures, the ticket goes to deep human review and remains visible.

**Invariant outcome:** the LLM never changes production or closes a ticket.
